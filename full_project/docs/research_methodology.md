# Research methodology

1. Define the question, geography, population, unit and time window.
2. Prefer primary sources such as INSEE, Eurostat, ECB and documented government releases.
3. Preserve source URL, retrieval timestamp, original payload where lawful and checksum.
4. Separate measured observations, estimates, news claims and forecasts.
5. Check units, seasonal adjustment, base periods, missingness, duplicates and revisions.
6. For reported events, record date, place, affected group, direct source, corroboration, status and uncertainty. RSS headlines are leads, not verification.
7. Treat food price inflation separately from physical availability and household food insecurity.
8. Estimate losses only with transparent scope, counterfactual, assumptions, period, source, uncertainty and overlap group. Avoid double counting.
9. Use time-based forecast validation and compare against simple baselines.
10. Publish limitations and uncertainty beside every conclusion.

Claim statuses should be explicit (for example unverified, partially corroborated, corroborated, disputed or outdated). Source counts are not probabilities of truth.
