# Limitations and interpretation safeguards

1. The dashboard does not automatically certify a national crisis. Evidence must be triangulated across economic indicators, official releases, and reliable reporting.
2. Inflation measures price changes, not physical shortages, food insecurity, or individual hardship.
3. Household income measures vary by definition, population, tax treatment, and release period.
4. Exchange rates are affected by euro-area-wide and global factors; avoid attributing changes solely to France.
5. A naive forecast repeats the latest observation and is only a baseline. It is not a policy forecast and has no causal interpretation.
6. Source availability, revisions, API changes, and release lags can make a dashboard stale. Display reference dates and validate units.
7. News and social reports can be incomplete or contested. Keep a claim ledger with URLs, publication dates, evidence status, and notes.
8. Estimates of losses or damage must identify the method, assumptions, time period, and uncertainty; do not add overlapping estimates.
